# Restaurant AI Council Baseline

This baseline maps the seven active personas for the restaurant industry package.

## Persona Set

1. Multi-site Restaurant Manager  
   Focus: operational control, speed, compliance confidence.
2. Back-office Admin  
   Focus: auditability, exports, policy enforcement, repeatability.
3. External Hospitality Consultant  
   Focus: scalability, adoption risk, measurable ROI.
4. Career Hospitality Professional  
   Focus: practical workflow fit during live service.
5. Fast-food Entry Worker  
   Focus: language access, onboarding clarity, confidence to execute.
6. Low-literacy Worker  
   Focus: dignity-preserving UX, visual-first interaction, minimal text reliance.
7. Sommelier / High-education Specialist  
   Focus: depth, quality, advanced workflow fidelity.

## Persona-to-Risk Notes

- Manager: high risk if workflows take too long or hide live staffing gaps.
- Back-office admin: high risk if exports, audit traces, or policy status are weak.
- Consultant: high risk if setup cannot scale or measure ROI across sites.
- Professional operator: high risk if flow breaks under live service pressure.
- Entry worker: high risk if onboarding is language-heavy and confidence drops.
- Low-literacy worker: high risk if dignity-preserving interaction is absent.
- Specialist: high risk if advanced quality workflows are over-simplified.

## Persona-to-Template Priority

- Manager -> scheduling, incident, dashboard, staffing templates.
- Admin -> policy, reporting, compliance, audit templates.
- Consultant -> rollout, comparison, and KPI templates.
- Professional -> shift operation and handover templates.
- Entry worker -> onboarding and guided-task templates.
- Low-literacy worker -> visual-first task and confirmation templates.
- Specialist -> advanced training and service-quality templates.

## What this council governs

- Workflow assumptions in templates
- Default policy readability and actionability
- Journey step complexity and sequencing
- Required localization and accessibility patterns
- Test scenario diversity (happy path plus realistic failure path)

## Expected Test Segments

- Operational speed segment
- Compliance-heavy segment
- Low-language and low-literacy segment
- Specialist-quality segment
- New-hire uncertainty segment

This segmentation should be used when defining both A/B and security test scenarios.
