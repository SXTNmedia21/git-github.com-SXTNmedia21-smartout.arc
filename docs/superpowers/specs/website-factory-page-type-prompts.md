# Website Factory — Page Type Design Prompts

> 20 production-ready design prompts for hospitality website page types. Each prompt includes: page purpose, target audience, visual direction, layout structure, sections/components, interaction behavior, copy tone, conversion goal, and design constraints.
>
> These prompts are structured to be usable by: AI website builders, UI/UX designers, front-end developers, no-code builders (Bubble, Framer, Webflow AI, v0, Lovable, Cursor).

---

## Master Prompt Formula

```
Design a high-conversion, brand-authentic web page for a [restaurant type / hospitality concept].
The page should be optimized for [primary goal], targeting [audience].
Use a visual direction that feels [adjectives], with [color/material/photography guidance].
Build the page with a clear hierarchy including [key sections].
Include components such as [components].
Interactions should feel [interaction style], with strong usability on mobile.
The copy tone should be [tone].
Prioritize conversion toward [main CTA], with secondary support for [secondary CTA].
Avoid [bad patterns / stylistic mistakes].
```

### Stacking order for best output:

1. Page type
2. Audience
3. Business goal
4. Visual mood
5. Required sections
6. Interaction rules
7. Mobile behavior
8. Brand constraints
9. Conversion priority

### Universal design instruction:

> Create a hospitality website page with clear visual hierarchy, premium typography, strong emotional positioning, conversion-first CTA placement, mobile-first responsiveness, and realistic restaurant UX patterns. Design for trust, appetite, atmosphere, and action. Avoid template-like layouts, generic stock composition, weak CTA hierarchy, and decorative clutter. Every section should serve either desire, clarity, or conversion.

## 1. Fine Dining Restaurant Homepage

**Tier:** Premium

**Instruction:**
Design a premium homepage for a fine dining restaurant that communicates exclusivity, culinary craftsmanship, and emotional atmosphere. The design should feel editorial, cinematic, and understated rather than loud or commercial.

Use a refined visual language with elegant typography, generous white space, dark muted tones, warm neutrals, rich food photography, and subtle motion. The user should feel like they are entering a curated dining experience, not just browsing a restaurant website.

**Goals:**

- Build desire and prestige
- Encourage table reservations
- Present menu, chef story, and ambience with sophistication

**Layout:**

- Full-screen hero with cinematic restaurant image or slow-moving video
- Minimal top navigation with logo, menu, reservations, about, contact
- Strong reservation CTA visible in header and hero
- Short brand statement beneath hero
- Featured tasting menu or signature dishes section
- Chef philosophy/story section
- Gallery section with immersive editorial layout
- Testimonials or press mentions
- Location, opening hours, and booking module in footer

**Components:**

- Sticky header with subtle transparency
- Elegant reservation button
- Split-layout content blocks
- High-end image grid with asymmetrical composition
- Press logos in monochrome
- Refined accordion or modal for menu preview

**Interaction:**

- Smooth scroll
- Slow fade-ins and slight parallax
- Hover effects must be subtle and luxurious
- Reservation CTA should remain accessible at all times
- Menu preview opens quickly without overwhelming the page

**Copy Tone:** Concise, elevated. Avoid clichés like "best food in town." Emphasize craftsmanship, seasonality, intimacy, and experience.

**Conversion:** Primary: reservation. Secondary: menu exploration.

**Constraints:** Do not make it look like a hotel booking site or nightlife venue. Avoid overly trendy neon, excessive gradients, or cluttered food cards.

## 2. Casual Bistro Homepage

**Tier:** Basic

**Instruction:**
Create a welcoming homepage for a neighborhood bistro that feels warm, human, social, and approachable. The design should convey comfort, everyday quality, and community connection.

**Goals:**

- Encourage reservations and walk-ins
- Showcase menu personality
- Make the restaurant feel familiar and trustworthy

**Visual Direction:** Warm earth tones, soft creams, muted reds, olive greens, handwritten accents, and authentic lifestyle photography. The design should feel lived-in rather than polished to perfection.

**Layout:**

- Hero with happy social dining moment
- Immediate display of cuisine type, location, and open status
- Reservation and view menu CTAs above the fold
- Popular dishes strip
- Story section about the neighborhood and concept
- Menu categories preview
- Weekly specials section
- Reviews section
- Map, hours, and contact

**Components:**

- Friendly typography pairing
- Rounded cards or soft edges
- Small icons for menu categories
- Repeating CTA blocks
- Instagram-style gallery
- Event/specials banner

**Interaction:**

- Hover states should feel light and friendly
- Allow quick access to menu and booking
- Mobile-first CTA placement
- Menu category tabs should be easy to scan

**Copy Tone:** Warm, conversational, down-to-earth, lightly playful.

**Conversion:** Table booking, menu browsing, and "get directions."

**Constraints:** Avoid luxury cues, overdesigned transitions, or generic stock imagery.

## 3. Modern Fast Casual Ordering Page

**Tier:** Basic Pro

**Instruction:**
Design a high-performance ordering page for a modern fast casual restaurant. The page must optimize speed, clarity, and conversion for takeout and delivery users.

**Goals:**

- Drive online orders fast
- Reduce ordering friction
- Make browsing simple on mobile

**Visual Direction:** Clean, bright, bold, energetic. Use strong contrast, appetite-triggering product photography, clear information hierarchy, and modern utility-first layout.

**Layout:**

- Compact hero with ordering-focused headline
- Location selection and service mode toggle: pickup or delivery
- Sticky cart summary
- Menu categories with filters
- Product cards with image, short description, price, tags
- Upsell modules for sides, drinks, combos
- Social proof or "most ordered" section
- Checkout CTA always visible

**Components:**

- Service mode switcher
- Dynamic menu cards
- Sticky mobile order bar
- "Add to cart" controls
- Nutrition/allergen tags
- Popular items carousel
- Combo builder

**Interaction:**

- Menu filters should be instant
- Add-to-cart flow must be frictionless
- Cart updates in real time
- Mobile sticky bottom CTA is mandatory
- Reordering previously selected items should be easy

**Copy Tone:** Direct, concise, energetic, utility-driven.

**Conversion:** Primary: online orders. Secondary: app downloads or loyalty signup.

**Constraints:** Do not bury the ordering flow in branding fluff. Function beats decoration.

## 5. Chef's Table Experience Page

**Tier:** Premium

**Instruction:**
Create a highly immersive landing page for a chef's table or tasting experience. The design should feel intimate, rare, and story-driven, with a strong sense of anticipation.

**Goals:**

- Sell limited-seat reservations
- Communicate exclusivity and narrative dining
- Build emotional tension and desirability

**Visual Direction:** Dark mode or deep luxurious palette. Cinematic visuals. Sparse layout. Strong focus on food detail, atmosphere, chef presence, and storytelling.

**Layout:**

- Dramatic hero with limited-seat message
- Section explaining the concept and what makes it special
- Multi-course journey visualization
- Chef narrative and culinary philosophy
- Experience details: duration, seating count, pricing, dietary notes
- FAQ
- Reservation CTA repeated multiple times
- Visual sequence of the evening

**Components:**

- Sticky reservation CTA
- Timeline of the meal progression
- Editorial quotes
- Limited availability callouts
- Elegant booking card
- Rich image storytelling modules

**Interaction:**

- Scroll-based narrative reveal
- Subtle transitions between courses
- Focus on immersion, not speed
- Reservation CTA should appear just before drop-off points

**Copy Tone:** Confident, restrained, poetic but precise.

**Conversion:** High-intent booking.

**Constraints:** Do not overcrowd the page with too much information upfront. Let mystery support conversion.
